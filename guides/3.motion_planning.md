## Key ideas

- **Configuration space:** one point represents the whole robot configuration. Obstacles become sets of forbidden configurations; include self-collisions. Workspace can be 3D while configuration space has many degrees of freedom.
- **Free path:** a continuous curve through collision-free configurations. Checking start and goal does not establish that the connecting motion is free.
- **PRM:** sample free configurations, connect feasible nearby pairs, then search a reusable roadmap. Particularly useful for repeated queries in a fixed environment.
- **RRT:** sample a target, find the nearest tree node, extend toward the target and check the connection. It explores outward from a start; larger unexplored regions tend to attract extensions. Goal bias and bidirectional search are variants, not new guarantees.
- **Ingredients:** sampler, metric/nearest-neighbor search, steering/local planner, collision checker. Their geometry and constraints must be compatible.
- **Guarantees:** complete means find a solution or report none in the problem setting. Probabilistically complete means success probability approaches one with unlimited sampling under suitable assumptions. It does not mean certain success within your time budget or shortest-path optimality.
- **Limits:** narrow passages receive few useful samples. Local trajectory optimization may remain in one route/homotopy class. Basic PRM/RRT need not be optimal; star variants add mechanisms for asymptotic optimality under assumptions.
- **Nonholonomic car:** no sideways velocity at an instant, yet a sequence of turns and forward/backward movements can produce sideways displacement. The local planner must respect that constraint.

**Connection:** planning explores possible routes; trajectory optimization refines feasible motion; feedback control executes and corrects it. Torque/contact constraints can invalidate a purely geometric route.

**Do not confuse:** a configuration with a physical point; probabilistic completeness with finite-time certainty; feasibility with optimality; an instantaneous velocity restriction with a ban on a final pose. Skip exact decomposition proofs and implementation details.

2. **P02.** What must a collision-free configuration account for?
3. **P03.** How does configuration-space dimension differ from the dimension of physical workspace?
4. **P04.** Why can a collision-free geometric path still be impossible to execute?
5. **P05.** What basic ingredients make sampling-based planning applicable to different robots?
6. **P06.** Why is PRM commonly useful for multiple planning queries?
7. **P07.** Why is RRT commonly useful for a single planning query?

9. **P09.** Why is checking only the endpoints of an edge insufficient?
10. **P10.** What does probabilistic completeness guarantee, and what does it not guarantee?
11. **P11.** How does a complete planner differ from a probabilistically complete one?
12. **P12.** Why are narrow passages difficult for sampling-based planners?
13. **P13.** Why can local trajectory optimization fail to discover a route around an obstacle?
14. **P14.** How can a car reach a sideways-displaced parking pose even though it cannot move sideways instantaneously?
15. **P15.** Do basic PRM/RRT guarantee the shortest path, and why introduce star variants?

## Answers

- **P01:** It parameterizes the robot's configuration, such as its joint values. One point describes the entire robot configuration, not one physical point on its surface.
- **P02:** All relevant robot geometry, environmental obstacles and self-collisions, not just the end-effector's position.
- **P03:** Workspace may be 3D while the robot has many joint degrees of freedom. Numerical representation size can also exceed intrinsic dimension.
- **P04:** Actuation, acceleration/torque, contact or velocity constraints can invalidate it. A geometric path alone is not a dynamically feasible motion.
- **P05:** A sampler, a distance/nearest-neighbor notion, a steering/local planner and collision checking, consistent with the configuration geometry and constraints.
- **P06:** It builds a reusable approximate roadmap of free space; different starts and goals can connect to that graph in a fixed environment.
- **P07:** It grows exploration from the initial configuration toward the goal rather than first building a broadly reusable roadmap.
- **P08:** Sample a target in the space, find the nearest existing tree node, and extend toward the target. Larger unexplored regions tend to attract extensions.
- **P09:** Two free endpoints can be connected by a segment that passes through an obstacle. The motion along the connection must be checked.
- **P10:** Under suitable assumptions, success probability tends to one with unbounded sampling when a feasible path exists. It is not a finite-time success or optimality guarantee.
- **P11:** A complete planner is guaranteed to find a path or report that none exists in its problem setting; a sampling planner generally cannot certify failure after a finite unsuccessful run.
- **P12:** They occupy small regions of the relevant free space, so useful samples and connections can be rare, particularly as dimension grows.
- **P13:** It improves an initial trajectory locally and may not explore another route/homotopy class. Global exploration and local refinement serve different purposes.
- **P14:** Its velocity is nonholonomically constrained, but sequences of feasible forward/backward turning motions can produce the net displacement.
- **P15:** Basic versions seek feasible connections, not general optimality. Suitable star variants add mechanisms such as rewiring to approach optimality under additional assumptions.
